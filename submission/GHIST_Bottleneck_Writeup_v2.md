## Summary

I fitted a single instantaneous decline in effective population size to a polarized site-frequency spectrum (SFS). The selected estimator uses grouped frequency classes and covariance weights estimated from alternating genomic blocks, with fixed 50% shrinkage toward a diagonal covariance matrix. The submitted estimates are **1,961.3 generations since the decline** and **0.765468 for the ratio of current to ancestral effective population size**. The latter denotes approximately 76.55% retained population size, or a 23.45% reduction.

The official Final relative root mean squared error (RRMSE) was **0.7227318443058701**. This official result is distinct from the smaller errors observed on the public Testing dataset and local simulations. The simulated validation does not establish the accuracy of the Final estimates.

## Data and preprocessing

The official fits used the organizer's Testing VCF, [syn74933321, version 2](https://www.synapse.org/Synapse:syn74933321.2), and Final VCF, [syn74933318, version 3](https://www.synapse.org/Synapse:syn74933318.3). Each dataset contains 57 diploid individuals, corresponding to 114 sampled chromosome copies, across a 100,000,000 bp chromosome. The retained counts were 1,194,350 polymorphic records for Testing and 1,588,748 for Final. Input MD5 checksums were compared with the stored Synapse manifests.

Records were required to be biallelic SNPs with complete diploid genotypes and FILTER equal to PASS or “.”. Derived allele counts were determined from the ancestral-allele tag (AA): ALT copies were counted when AA matched REF, and the count was reversed when AA matched ALT. All retained records in these two releases had AA equal to REF. Independently recomputed ALT-count spectra were checked against bcftools output; the polarized block spectra were generated by the Python parser.

All eligible records were retained, including records sharing a genomic position. A separate sensitivity analysis retained only the last eligible record at each position. For Testing, that convention reproduced the organizer's example SFS exactly. It was not the convention used for the submitted prediction.

The 113 polymorphic derived-frequency classes were pooled into 14 bins with inclusive upper boundaries of 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 14, 21, 32, 46, 60, 79, 99, and 113. The chromosome was divided into 100 consecutive 1 Mb blocks.

## Demographic model and estimation

The model has constant ancestral and current effective population sizes separated by one instantaneous decline. It does not fit subsequent recovery, population structure, migration, or selection. The fitted size ratio is the current size divided by the ancestral size; effective population sizes are not census counts.

The implementation calculates the expected one-population SFS using a tridiagonal neutral-drift operator and its eigendecomposition. It evaluates the transient departure from the equilibrium spectrum after the size change. This is a custom Python implementation, with a separate R implementation for numerical comparison. It does not invoke dadi or the moments software package for fitting.

Let the fitted ratio be f and the scaled time be tau, where time in generations is 2 × current Ne × tau. The optimizer searches f from 0.0001 to 1 and tau from 0.00001 to 20 in log space. For a block of length L, the ancestral mutation scale is theta = 4 × ancestral Ne × mu × L, using the organizer-supplied mutation rate mu = 3.42 × 10⁻⁸ per base per generation.

For covariance-weighted fitting, alternating blocks form two groups of 50. Each group's mean spectrum is weighted by the inverse covariance estimated from the other group. Each covariance is replaced by 0.5 times the empirical covariance plus 0.5 times its diagonal matrix. One demographic model is fitted to both means by minimizing the sum of the two quadratic residual terms. The mutation scale is profiled analytically for each ratio and scaled time. This is a generalized least-squares criterion, not a full linked-site likelihood; alternating blocks are not assumed to eliminate linkage.

The optimization combines three L-BFGS-B starts with a parameter grid and independent Nelder–Mead refinement. Finite solutions, objective agreement, and search boundaries are checked. The R verification script fits ancestral Ne, current Ne, and time directly and checks agreement with the Python result within 0.02 generations and 0.000001 in the size ratio.

## Results and validation

**Table 1. Official datasets and parameter estimates.** Testing error is calculated locally against the public answer. Final RRMSE is the official challenge score; the Final parameter truths are not available in this repository.

| Dataset / result | Generations since decline | Current / ancestral Ne | RRMSE and source |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Public Testing answer | 4,757 | 0.52 | Reference values |
| Testing, selected fixed-shrinkage GLS | 4,598.6 | 0.521876 | 0.0236853, calculated locally |
| Final, submitted fixed-shrinkage GLS | **1,961.3** | **0.765468** | **0.7227318443058701**, official score |

For local comparisons, the error for each replicate is the square root of the mean of the squared relative errors in time and size ratio. Pooled RMSE is calculated from squared errors across replicates, rather than by averaging replicate RMSEs.

Initial selection compared the folded-SFS Poisson baseline with the fixed-shrinkage polarized GLS estimator on 80 simulated genomes: four demographic scenarios with 20 replicates each. Each genome consisted of 1,000 independent 25 kb loci simulated with Hudson's ms through phyclust, totaling 25 Mb. Ten loci were combined into each statistical block. The mutation rate was 3.42 × 10⁻⁸ and the recombination rate was 1.87 × 10⁻⁸ per base per generation. Pooled relative RMSE was 11.85% for the folded baseline and 6.77% for fixed GLS. The implemented selection rule required improvement on Testing and pooled simulations, improvement in at least three of four simulated scenarios, and an interior Testing solution. Fixed GLS met these criteria. Because the public Testing answer contributed to selection, its error is not an independent test of generalization.

A second comparison used 40 independently simulated continuous 100 Mb chromosomes, comprising four new scenarios with 10 replicates each. These were generated with scrm 1.7.5 using a 10 kb pruning window. This approximation differs from an exact ancestral recombination graph simulation. The five methods below used matched inputs.

**Table 2. Local simulation results on 40 continuous 100 Mb chromosomes.** Values are percentages of relative RMSE, not official challenge scores.

| Estimator | Pooled, both parameters | Time | Size ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Folded Poisson | 8.30% | 11.64% | 1.53% |
| Polarized Poisson, 113 classes | 5.91% | 8.24% | 1.40% |
| Polarized Poisson, 14 bins | 5.92% | 8.26% | 1.40% |
| Fixed-shrinkage GLS, submitted estimator | 6.32% | 8.83% | 1.45% |
| Adaptive-shrinkage GLS, candidate | 6.29% | 8.78% | 1.44% |

In this second comparison, the two polarized Poisson estimators had lower pooled error than fixed GLS. The results therefore do not support a claim that covariance weighting is uniformly superior. The second-stage replacement rule considered adaptive GLS as the challenger to the incumbent. Adaptive GLS improved two of four scenarios and 18 of 40 individual replicates. Its relative reduction in pooled RMSE was 0.53%, with a scenario-stratified paired bootstrap 95% interval of −1.71% to +3.03% from 10,000 draws. It met neither the positive lower-bound requirement nor the requirement to improve at least three scenarios, so fixed GLS was retained.

Keeping only the last record per Final position changed the inferred time to 1,931.1 generations and the size ratio to approximately 0.765769. Deleting consecutive 10 Mb segments and refitting gave times of 1,853–2,073 generations and ratios of 0.7592–0.7707. Corresponding block-jackknife standard errors were approximately 204 generations and 0.0112 in the ratio. These are within-dataset sensitivity measures, not calibrated confidence intervals for the hidden truth. They do not account for all model misspecification or explain the substantially larger official Final error.

## Code, reproducibility, and limitations

The code and archived summary tables are in [candicewu0515/GHIST](https://github.com/candicewu0515/GHIST). The [reproduction guide](../REPRODUCIBILITY.md) documents input versions, required filenames, software, and commands that do not regenerate the locked prediction file. The original brief draft is retained separately.

The repository contains source code, input manifests, summary statistics, and the historical prediction artifact. It does not distribute the challenge VCFs or raw data. Access to the input data must be obtained separately through the organizer. Repository access is currently private, so this link alone does not make the methods publicly accessible or satisfy the Synapse writeup submission requirement.

The main limitations are the restricted two-epoch model, reliance on correct ancestral states and absolute mutation/length calibration, sensitivity to repeated-coordinate conventions, limited simulation scenarios, and approximate linkage simulation. The final official score should be used when describing challenge performance. No rank, award, or consortium-level authorship is claimed by this document.
